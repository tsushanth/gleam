## 1. Market Analysis

### 1.1 Market Size & Growth

The household cleaning and chore-tracking app space sits at an intersection of several larger markets. Estimates vary by definition scope:

| Definition | 2024–2025 Value | 2033 Projection | CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cleaning schedule/routine apps (narrow) | $330M (2025) | $610M | 7.8% |
| Home management apps (broad) | $1.2B (2024) | $3.5B | 12.5% |
| Habit-tracking apps (closest proxy) | $11.42B (2024) | $14.94B (2026) | 14.41% |
| Productivity apps category (iOS) | $14.46B (2026) | $30.85B (2034) | 9.94% |

Key drivers:
- **Dual-income households** with smartphones seeking cognitive offload tools
- **Mental health awareness** — 61% of users cite habit formation for mental well-being as a primary motivation
- **North America** accounts for ~41% of market share
- **Schedule Optimization** features hold the largest adoption share at 38%
- **Smart home integration** is driving 29% of feature adoption growth
- The App Store is booming in 2026, largely attributed to the AI app wave (TechCrunch, April 2026)

### 1.2 Growth Trends

- Productivity apps moved into the **top 5 categories** for new app releases in 2026
- AI-powered apps are the #1 growth vector across all app categories
- The "clean girl aesthetic" was one of the top cultural trends of 2025 (Euronews)
- **#CleanTok** has surpassed 150 billion TikTok views — cleaning has become a lifestyle and self-care category, not just a utility
- Neurodivergent users (ADHD, autism spectrum) represent a rapidly growing, underserved niche in habit and routine apps

### 1.3 User Demographics

**Primary audience:**
- Adults 25–45, primarily female-identifying (aligns with household management cultural patterns, though this is shifting)
- Dual-income households — both partners working, cognitive load of home management is high
- Families with children — need delegation, accountability, and kid-friendly incentives
- Apartment dwellers and first-time homeowners who are building cleaning routines for the first time

**Emerging segments:**
- **ADHD/neurodivergent users** — explicitly seeking structure-without-rigidity; a large and vocal underserved market
- **Gen Z "clean girl" adopters** — inspired by CleanTok; cleaning as self-care, not obligation
- **Roommate households** — need transparent task delegation without a designated "household manager"
- **Senior users** — interested in maintenance tracking for home ownership (gutters, filters, seasonal tasks)

### 1.4 What Makes Apps Succeed in This Space

From studying Tody (4.83 stars, 10k+ reviews after 13 years) and Sweepy (the fastest-growing alternative):

1. **Philosophy-first design**: Apps that encode a coherent worldview (Tody's "condition-based, not deadline-based") build passionate user communities. The concept must be explainable in one sentence.
2. **Visual feedback that creates satisfaction**: Color-changing bars, animations on task completion, visible "before/after" states — these are non-negotiable for retention.
3. **Guilt elimination**: Users are overwhelmed and behind. Apps that reframe "falling behind" as "dirtiness is just the state of things" are profoundly valued.
4. **Progressive complexity**: Simple to start (set up a room, add tasks), deep enough to customize. Users who configure the app to their specific home have much higher retention (sunk cost + personal relevance).
5. **Household sync**: Multi-user features unlock the family market and significantly increase willingness to pay.
6. **ADHD-friendly design**: Flexible deadlines, visual rather than text-only tracking, and satisfying completion interactions are praised by the neurodivergent community regardless of whether the app explicitly targets them.

### 1.5 Retention Challenges

The category's biggest business problem:

- **77% of new daily active users are gone by day 3**
- **21% abandon after just one session**
- Monthly churn is 5.5% in 2026, more than double the 2.2% benchmark from 2019
- The #1 cause: activation failure — users never form a habit in the first 7 days
- Users who return from vacation to find every task "red" often churn permanently (Tody calls this the "chore debt" abandonment pattern)

## 2. Competitor Deep Dive

### 2.1 Tody — Primary Competitor

**Overview:**
- Developer: looploop (Iceland)
- Platforms: iOS and Android
- Active since ~2012 (App Store ID 595339588 suggests early placement)
- Rating: 4.83/5, 10,000+ US reviews
- 1M+ active users

#### The Tody Method (Core Intellectual Property)

Tody's foundational innovation is **condition-based tracking** rather than calendar-based scheduling:
- Each task has a "frequency" (e.g., "vacuum every 7 days")
- Instead of showing a due date, Tody shows a visual bar that drains from green → yellow → red as days pass
- There are no "overdue" labels — just urgency indicators
- The psychological shift: "I'm not behind — the bathroom is just a 7 on the dirtiness scale"
- Users with ADHD in particular cite this as removing paralysis and guilt

This is the feature Gleam **must** replicate or meaningfully surpass. It is Tody's most defensible and most-loved feature.

#### Current Feature Set (2025–2026)

**Core (free):**
- Room/Zone organization (Kitchen, Bathroom, Bedroom, Whole House, Garage, Outdoors)
- Custom task setup with frequency control
- "Last done" timestamp tracking
- Auto-prioritized task list (green → yellow → red urgency order)
- "Just Did It" button (marks as done without completing — resets the timer)
- Basic notifications/reminders

**Premium (subscription, ~$30/year for family):**
- Cross-device cloud sync
- Multi-user household accounts with task assignment
- Automated rotation among household members
- FairShare (2025) — labor distribution balancing between partners
- iOS widgets
- Premium gamification: points, levels, trophies, streaks
- Dusty — virtual dust bunny character for kid-friendly household engagement
- Monthly challenges and unlockable rewards
- Eco-Tips (2025) — green cleaning alternatives per task

#### Pricing History

- **2012–2024:** One-time paid purchase on iOS (~$3.99); free with ads on Android
- **Late 2024 → present:** Freemium model with subscription tiers (Solo, Duo, Family, Team)
- Current pricing: ~$30/year for family sync tier
- Transition generated limited public pushback; free tier retained sufficient core functionality

#### What Users Love

1. **The dirtiness meter** — the #1 cited differentiator; "I don't feel guilty for the first time"
2. **Customizability** — used for pet care, maintenance, seasonal tasks, not just cleaning
3. **ADHD-suitability** — routine structure without rigid deadline anxiety; praised by neurodivergent users
4. **Gamification with Dusty** — particularly effective for families with kids
5. **Non-aggressive paywall** — free tier is genuinely functional
6. **Mental load reduction** — "it lives in the app, not my head"
7. **FairShare** (2025) — first real attempt to address equitable household labor distribution

#### What Users Complain About (Documented Gaps)

| Pain Point | Severity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| No search function | High | Explicitly requested for years; not built; painful at scale |
| No manual task ordering | High | Users cannot reorder tasks within an area |
| No per-task notification control | Medium | Can't set individual notification times per task |
| Multi-person coordination is weak | High | FairShare is surface-level; not purpose-built for delegation |
| UI feels dated | Medium | Visual design hasn't kept pace with iOS standards |
| History graph takes too much space | Low | Users prefer seeing task notes/instructions |
| Limited color customization | Low | Users want visual personalization |
| Task completion sounds unsatisfying | Low | The "click" is weak; users want more reward |
| "Chore debt" on return from vacation | Medium | No smart handling of absence periods |
| Can't assign one task to multiple rooms | Medium | Annoying for multi-bathroom homes |

#### Strategic Weakness

Tody is **designed for the solo household manager** who runs a system and then communicates with others separately. It excels at giving one person a complete view of a home's cleaning state. Family/roommate coordination is bolted on rather than foundational — FairShare shows awareness of the problem but the architecture wasn't built for true multi-person accountability.

## 4. UX Patterns

### 4.1 Onboarding Flow Best Practices

The biggest drop-off risk is activation failure. Data:
- Average time-to-value benchmark: 60 seconds for AI-powered apps; ~10 minutes for configuration-heavy apps
- Apps requiring extensive setup before value delivery lose 55%+ of new users
- Progress bars during onboarding increase completion by 20–30%
- A two-question welcome survey is the sweet spot — more questions cause drop-off before onboarding starts

**Recommended Gleam Onboarding Architecture:**

1. **Welcome (1 screen):** Value prop + visual. "Your home, always just clean enough. Zero guilt."
2. **Two-question segmentation (1 screen):**
   - "Who are you cleaning for?" (Just me / Me + partner / Family / Roommates)
   - "What's your biggest cleaning challenge?" (Forgetting tasks / Motivation / Fair division / Where to start)
3. **AI routine generation offer (1 screen):** "Let AI build your first cleaning plan in 60 seconds" — clear CTA, skip option
4. **Home setup (2–3 screens):** Room selection (visual tiles, tap to include), basic task defaults per room (pre-populated, user can edit)
5. **First "aha!" moment (1 screen):** Show the dirtiness visualization for their home for the first time — color bars already meaningful
6. **Paywall (soft):** After first value moment, before multi-device or sharing features
7. **Notification permission:** Asked only after user has seen the app's value; never on first launch

### 4.2 Navigation Structure

Standard pattern in this category:

- **Bottom Tab Bar:** Home/Dashboard | Rooms | Tasks/Today | Progress | Settings
- **Dashboard:** The "command center" — overall home health, most urgent tasks, today's focus
- **Room View:** Card-based room list → tap to expand tasks within that room
- **Task Detail:** Task name, frequency, last done, dirtiness level, notes, assignment
- **Progress/Stats:** History charts, streaks, household leaderboard
- **Floating Action Button:** Quick task entry or "Just Did It" shortcut

Tody uses a drawer/sidebar pattern; this feels dated vs. iOS tab bar conventions. Gleam should use native iOS tab bar.

### 4.3 Data Presentation Patterns

**The dirtiness visualization** is the category's most important data display pattern:
- Color gradient (green → yellow → orange → red) on a pill/capsule shape
- Should be readable at a glance in list view without opening task
- Percentage or days-until-red as secondary label
- Rooms/areas should display aggregate dirtiness (worst task in the room represents room health)

**Task list views:**
- Sort by urgency (default) — most urgent at top
- Group by room (alternative view)
- Filter by assignee (in multi-user mode)
- Swipe-to-complete gesture (iOS convention)

**Progress views:**
- Weekly/monthly bar charts per room
- Streak counter prominently displayed
- "Tasks completed this week" stat
- Household comparison (who did what this week)

### 4.4 Key User Journeys

**Journey 1: Daily cleaning check-in (most frequent)**
1. Open app or check widget
2. See today's urgent tasks highlighted
3. Tap a task → mark as done → satisfying animation/sound
4. Progress bar fills, dirtiness bar resets to green
5. Close app; next open in 1–3 days

**Journey 2: Planning a cleaning session**
1. "I have 30 minutes to clean" — enter time budget
2. AI suggests task sequence fitting the time budget
3. User works through list; tasks auto-prioritized by urgency and location (kitchen tasks before bathroom tasks)
4. Session summary: tasks completed, time spent, points earned

**Journey 3: Family delegation**
1. View household task list filtered by "unassigned"
2. Assign tasks to family members
3. Family member gets notification
4. Family member completes + logs
5. Assigner sees completion in real-time
6. Points/streaks updated for both

**Journey 4: New home setup (onboarding)**
1. AI generates suggested routine based on home size + occupants
2. User reviews and edits suggested tasks
3. First dirtiness visualization displayed
4. App is immediately useful without manual configuration

### 4.5 Interaction Design Patterns

- **Swipe left to complete:** The dominant task completion gesture; do not deviate
- **Haptic feedback on completion:** Strong haptic (UIImpactFeedbackGenerator.heavy) on task done
- **ASMR-friendly sounds:** Satisfying completion chime, "cleaning" sounds as tasks complete — this is a known TikTok-shareable feature
- **Contextual menus (long press):** Edit, reassign, skip, snooze
- **Drag to reorder:** Within room task lists — Tody's glaring gap
- **Pull to refresh:** Sync state refresh
- **Undo shake:** Accidental completion recovery

**Gleam planned pricing:**
- Weekly: $3.19
- Monthly: $7.99
- Yearly: $63.99
- Lifetime: $127.98
- Remove Ads IAP: $1.99

**Assessment:** Gleam's monthly ($7.99) is below Tody ($9.99) but the yearly ($63.99) is close to Tody ($79.99) — which is unusual. Most users compare monthly → annual savings. At $63.99/year vs. $7.99/month, the annual discount is ~33%, which is less compelling than the industry standard 40–50%. Consider adjusting to $39.99–$49.99/year to match Sweepy's positioning.

### 5.2 What Users Will Pay For vs. Expect Free

**Universally expected free:**
- Basic task lists (1 home, limited rooms)
- Core room/zone setup (up to 3 rooms)
- Dirtiness visualization (the core mechanism)
- Single-device functionality
- Basic reminders
- Manual task logging

**Users pay for:**
- Cloud sync across devices (strongest driver of subscription)
- Multi-user household sharing (family plan differentiator)
- AI features — routine generation, coaching, smart reminders
- Advanced analytics and history
- Premium gamification (leaderboard, streaks, seasonal challenges)
- Additional homes/properties
- Ad removal
- Widgets

### 5.3 Free Tier Strategy

Tody's success is partly attributable to a **genuinely generous free tier**. Users who configure the app for their specific home are far more likely to convert (sunk cost + personal relevance) than users who hit a hard paywall before seeing value.

**Recommended free tier for Gleam:**
- 1 home, up to 5 rooms, unlimited tasks
- Dirtiness visualization + full urgency tracking
- Single-user, single-device
- Basic completion history (last 30 days)
- 1 AI routine generation per month (teaser)
- No ads if possible (ads in a cleaning routine app feel friction-adding)

### 5.4 Subscription Fatigue & Lifetime Purchase Opportunity

- One-time/lifetime purchases grew from 6.4% to 10.3% of app monetization models between 2023 and 2025
- 35% of apps now combine subscriptions with lifetime purchase options
- There is a vocal, high-intent user segment (visible on Product Hunt, Reddit) that will pay significantly more for a one-time price than they would subscribe
- Tody doesn't currently offer a lifetime option — this is a direct opportunity
- Gleam's $127.98 lifetime is priced appropriately for the segment (2× annual = breakeven at year 2)

### 5.5 Paywall Placement Strategy

- **Soft paywall after first value moment** — not on first launch
- Trigger on: attempting to add a second device, inviting a household member, or accessing AI coaching
- Show what the premium version looks like (locked feature previews) before asking for payment
- Free trial: 7-day free trial of full Premium — "try before you buy" reduces subscription anxiety
- Annual plan as the default selection on the paywall (not monthly)

### 5.6 Revenue Growth Levers Post-Launch

1. **Referral program:** "Invite a household member, both get 1 month free"
2. **Seasonal promotions:** Spring cleaning campaign (March/April), New Year resolutions (January)
3. **Bundle with lifetime:** "Launch special — lifetime access at 50% off, first 500 users"
4. **Content hooks:** App-generated shareable "cleaning score" cards for TikTok/Instagram Stories

## 6. Differentiation Opportunities

### 6.1 Core Differentiation Pillars for Gleam

Based on the competitive analysis, Gleam's unique position should rest on three pillars:

**Pillar 1: AI-First from Day One**
Not as a feature bolt-on, but as the foundational experience. Tody requires manual setup; Gleam should offer "your complete cleaning routine in 60 seconds" powered by Claude. This is the clearest perception gap — Tody is a well-designed spreadsheet; Gleam is a smart cleaning partner.

**Pillar 2: Built for Modern Households (Not Solo Household Managers)**
Tody's architecture assumes one person runs the show. Gleam assumes labor is shared, even if imperfectly. True multi-user equity — visible workloads, assignment, completion accountability, and fair rotation — built into the data model from the start.

**Pillar 3: CleanTok-Native Aesthetic**
The visual language of the app should align with the clean girl aesthetic: soft palettes, satisfying micro-animations, ASMR completion sounds, shareable progress moments. No competitor has treated TikTok virality as a design constraint. Gleam should.

### 6.2 Specific Feature Gaps to Exploit

| Tody Gap | Gleam Solution |
|---|---|
| No search within app | Full-text search across all tasks, rooms, and notes |
| No manual task ordering | Drag-to-reorder within any room |
| No per-task notification control | Individual reminder time per task |
| Weak multi-person experience | Purpose-built household coordination with real accountability |
| No AI at all | Claude-powered routine generation, coaching, smart prioritization |
| No voice input | Hands-free "just did it" via voice |
| Dated UI | iOS 26-native design, clean girl aesthetic, ASMR micro-interactions |
| No lifetime purchase | $127.98 lifetime — captures anti-subscription segment Tody ignores |
| Chore debt on return from vacation | Smart absence scaling (proportional urgency adjustment, not full red) |

### 6.3 AI Differentiation Specifics (Claude Integration)

The Claude API is the primary differentiator engine. Specific use cases ranked by user excitement and implementation feasibility:

1. **Onboarding routine generator (HIGH priority):**
   Prompt flow: User answers 3–5 questions (home size, occupant count, pets, lifestyle pace, priorities). Claude returns a complete, editable cleaning plan with tasks, rooms, and frequencies. Immediate value; replaces 30 minutes of manual setup.

2. **AI cleaning coach (HIGH priority):**
   Weekly summary delivered as a conversational insight: "Your kitchen needed cleaning 4 times this week — 2× the rest of your home. This is typical for households with X. Here's a tip to reduce that." Makes the app feel alive and personalized over time.

3. **Smart task prioritization (MEDIUM priority):**
   User inputs "I have 45 minutes and medium energy" — Claude returns a sequenced task list optimized for time, location (room clustering), and urgency.

4. **Fair distribution AI (MEDIUM priority):**
   Analyzes historical completion data per household member and suggests rebalancing. "Alex has done 73% of bathroom cleaning this month. Consider reassigning the toilet scrub to Sam."

5. **Voice completion (MEDIUM priority):**
   Speech-to-text integration — user says "I just cleaned the kitchen counters and the stovetop" — Claude parses and logs both tasks.

6. **Photo room scan (LOW priority for v1, HIGH priority for v2):**
   Vision AI identifies tasks from a photo of a room. High user excitement but technically complex for v1. Use AVFoundation + a vision model API.

### 6.4 Target Audience Positioning

**Primary:** The "clean girl aesthetic" Gen Z and millennial woman who follows CleanTok, wants her home to reflect her values, and is frustrated that Tody looks like it was designed in 2012.

**Secondary:** The ADHD household — specifically adults with ADHD who have tried and failed with rigid cleaning schedules and need condition-based tracking without guilt spirals.

**Tertiary:** The equitable household — couples and roommates who want chores to feel fair and want the app to back them up when "it's your turn to clean the bathroom."

### 6.5 Marketing Differentiation

- **TikTok-first organic strategy:** Design the app to generate shareable moments (ASMR completion animations, before/after room cleanliness scores, household leaderboard screenshots)
- **Creator partnerships:** Reach out to CleanTok creators (Kristy Scott: 16M followers; DeclutterCore™ creator: 900K) for sponsored integrations
- **ADHD community:** Reddit communities (r/ADHD, r/adhdwomen) have documented Tody as a popular recommendation — position Gleam as the modern, AI-powered successor
- **"Gleam Score" social mechanic:** Shareable weekly home cleanliness score card for Instagram Stories/TikTok — free word-of-mouth vector
- **Keyword targeting:** "Tody alternative 2026," "cleaning app ADHD," "AI cleaning routine"

### 7.2 Data Model Architecture

**Core entities:**

```
Home
├── id: UUID
├── name: String
├── ownerUserID: String
└── members: [HouseholdMember]

Room
├── id: UUID
├── homeID: UUID
├── name: String
├── icon: String (SF Symbol name)
└── tasks: [CleaningTask]

CleaningTask
├── id: UUID
├── roomID: UUID
├── name: String
├── frequencyDays: Int              // How often task should be done
├── lastCompletedAt: Date?
├── completedByUserID: String?
├── assignedToUserID: String?
├── notes: String?
├── estimatedMinutes: Int?
├── isActive: Bool
└── pausedUntil: Date?             // Vacation/pause handling

CompletionLog
├── id: UUID
├── taskID: UUID
├── completedAt: Date
├── completedByUserID: String
└── notes: String?

HouseholdMember
├── userID: String
├── displayName: String
├── avatarColor: String
├── totalPoints: Int
└── weeklyPoints: Int
```

**Dirtiness calculation:**
```
dirtinessScore = (daysSinceLastCleaned / frequencyDays) * 100
// 0–49: green
// 50–74: yellow
// 75–89: orange
// 90+: red
```

This is Tody's core algorithm, reproduced from observation. The key insight is that dirtiness is a continuous gradient, not a binary due/overdue state.

### 7.3 CloudKit vs. Firebase Decision

For Gleam (iOS-first, potential Android later):

- **CloudKit:** Native to Apple ecosystem, free storage tiers, zero auth friction (uses iCloud), private database keeps user data siloed. Best choice for iOS-only v1. No Google account dependency.
- **Firebase:** Better for real-time multi-user scenarios (true websocket streaming), better Android/cross-platform support. Adds Google dependency and cost at scale.

**Recommendation:** Start with CloudKit + NSPersistentCloudKitContainer. It provides automatic sync with zero infrastructure cost and works natively with CoreData. If Android companion is added, migrate to a Firebase + shared backend model.

### 7.4 AI Integration (Claude API)

**Implementation pattern:**

- Claude API called server-side (do not embed API key in client)
- Build a lightweight backend (Cloudflare Workers or AWS Lambda) to proxy Claude API requests
- Cache routine generation results per user (routine doesn't change daily)
- Rate limit AI features by subscription tier (free tier: 1 generation/month; premium: unlimited coaching)

**Prompts to pre-engineer:**

1. Routine generator: takes home profile (rooms, occupants, pets, lifestyle, priorities) → returns structured JSON of tasks with frequencies
2. Weekly coach: takes completion data → returns 1–3 actionable insights in conversational tone
3. Session planner: takes time budget + urgency scores → returns ordered task list
4. Fair distribution analyzer: takes per-user completion logs → returns equity assessment

**Privacy consideration:** User home data (room names, task names, completion patterns) must be treated as personal data. Explicit consent required before sending to Claude API. Apple's App Review (since November 2025) requires a consent modal specifying the AI provider and data types before any sharing.

### 7.5 Offline Support

- All core functionality (task logging, dirtiness calculation, completion) must work fully offline
- CloudKit/Firebase sync handles reconciliation on reconnect
- Conflict resolution: Last-write-wins for task completion logs (acceptable for this domain)
- Local CoreData store is always the source of truth; cloud is the sync layer

### 7.6 Performance Considerations

- Dirtiness calculations for all tasks must be computed lazily (on display) not eagerly — homes can have 100+ tasks
- Widget timeline needs efficient background refresh; use WidgetKit's timeline provider to refresh at natural urgency transition points (when a task crosses green → yellow or yellow → red)
- Notification scheduling: use `UNCalendarNotificationTrigger` rather than time-interval triggers to handle vacation pause correctly
- Memory: avoid loading full completion history into memory; paginate logs

### 7.7 Accessibility Requirements

Per Apple HIG and App Store reviewer evaluation criteria:

- **VoiceOver:** Every visible text element must have an accessible label; all interactive elements must have meaningful descriptions; task urgency must be communicated via label, not color alone ("Kitchen — stovetop: needs cleaning soon" not just a red bar)
- **Dynamic Type:** All text must scale with iOS text size settings across 12 size categories (xSmall through AX5)
- **Color contrast:** Minimum 4.5:1 ratio for text; color is not the only indicator of state (patterns, labels, icons alongside color coding)
- **Touch targets:** Minimum 44×44 pt touch area for all interactive elements
- **Reduce Motion:** Honor `UIAccessibility.isReduceMotionEnabled` — disable ASMR animations when user has opted for reduced motion
- **Haptics toggle:** Let users disable haptic feedback in settings

## 8. Risk Assessment

### 8.1 App Store Rejection Risks

**Highest-risk areas for Gleam:**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| AI disclosure requirement missing | High | Required since November 2025: consent modal naming Claude and data types before any API call |
| Privacy manifest missing for 3rd-party SDKs | High | Every bundled SDK needs a privacy manifest; audit all dependencies pre-submission |
| Incomplete app at review | Medium | Ensure all screens are functional; no placeholder text, no "coming soon" screens |
| Insufficient functionality ("too simple") | Medium | Ensure feature depth is evident; Apple rejects apps that are basic web wrappers or trivially simple |
| Subscription paywall before meaningful value | Medium | Show value first; Apple rejects apps that paywall core functionality immediately |
| Missing Sign In with Apple | High | Required whenever any third-party login is offered; must be implemented at feature parity |
| iOS 26 SDK requirement | High (if not updated) | All new submissions from April 2026 must use iOS 26 SDK and Xcode 26 |

**40–60% of first-time iOS submissions are rejected.** However, 95% of rejections are from documented, avoidable causes.

### 8.2 Privacy Concerns

**Data collected and sensitivity:**
- Home layout (rooms) — LOW sensitivity
- Task names and frequencies — LOW sensitivity
- Completion timestamps — LOW sensitivity
- Household member relationships — MEDIUM sensitivity (who lives with whom)
- Location (if ever added for smart home detection) — HIGH sensitivity
- Photos (if room scan added) — HIGH sensitivity; requires explicit permission and careful handling

**Regulatory considerations:**
- GDPR (EU users): right to deletion, data portability, consent for AI processing
- CCPA (California users): right to know, delete, and opt-out of sale
- COPPA: If marketing to families with children under 13, extremely strict rules apply; ensure Dusty-style gamification does not attract under-13 users without explicit parental consent flow
- App Store privacy nutrition label (Privacy Manifests): must accurately declare all data types collected and purposes

**Recommendations:**
- Minimize data collection to what's functionally necessary
- Store task/home data in CloudKit private database (user-owned, Apple-mediated — the safest option for privacy optics)
- Never send raw room/task data to Claude API without explicit in-app consent modal
- Provide in-app data deletion ("Delete my account and all data") — required for App Store compliance

### 8.3 Content Issues

- No adult content concerns — cleaning app is entirely appropriate for all ages
- Gamification with kids (Dusty-equivalent): be cautious about creating an experience that requires parental consent under COPPA
- AI-generated text (coaching tips, routine suggestions): must not produce harmful, misleading, or embarrassing content — Claude's guardrails are sufficient but test edge cases (unusual room/task names that might produce weird suggestions)
- User-generated content (custom task names): minimal risk, but consider a basic profanity filter for tasks visible in shared household views

### 8.4 Competitive Risks

- **Tody copies the AI features:** Possible but unlikely to be fast; looploop is a small Iceland-based team, 13 years of technical debt, and their history shows slow feature velocity. A focused competitor can move faster.
- **Sweepy pivots to AI:** Already has predictive reminders; full AI coaching is a natural next step. Watch their 2026 updates.
- **Apple builds this into iOS:** Home app is a potential integration point. Low risk for specialized task tracking — Apple's Home app is IoT-focused, not cleaning-routine focused.
- **TikTok or Instagram builds a "clean challenge" feature:** Platform feature risk. Low probability; this is too niche for a platform play.
- **Category consolidation:** Larger productivity apps (Notion, Todoist) could add cleaning templates. Risk is real but cleaning requires domain-specific UX (dirtiness meters, rooms, household sync) that generic task managers don't deliver well.

### 8.5 Retention & Business Model Risks

- **Monthly churn at 5.5%** is the category norm — assume Gleam will face the same without intentional intervention
- **Activation failure** (77% of users gone by day 3) is the #1 business risk; onboarding quality is existential
- **Subscription fatigue:** Gleam's monthly price ($7.99) is high for the category; conversion rate from free to paid will be the key metric to watch
- **Competitive price pressure:** OurHome, Done, and Spotless are free. Gleam's free tier must be genuinely valuable or users will use free alternatives for basic needs
- **Android gap:** Tody has Android; Gleam's platform strategy should address this to avoid leaving the family segment half-served

### 8.6 Technical Risks

- **CloudKit limitations at scale:** CloudKit's shared database has rate limits and schema migration constraints; plan data model carefully upfront
- **Claude API latency:** AI coaching and routine generation must feel responsive; implement async loading with skeleton states, not blocking spinners
- **App size:** Large asset catalogs (ASMR sounds, animations, themed content) can bloat app size — keep under 50MB initial download, use on-demand resources
- **Widget reliability:** WidgetKit timelines must be accurate; stale dirtiness data in widgets is a trust-killer
- **Multi-user conflict resolution:** When two family members log the same task simultaneously, last-write-wins is usually fine — but design for it explicitly
